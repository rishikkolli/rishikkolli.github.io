Rules to keep

Non-negotiable
  • Don't run through pain. Stop if it worsens, changes your stride, or lingers between runs.
  • Rehearse fueling on every run over an hour, with the exact foods you'll carry.
  • No huge back-to-back weekend. The 12-miler is for gear, fueling and time on feet.
  • Don't make up missed miles. If the start moves closer, drop sessions instead of stacking them.
  • Have a bailout plan. If day one leaves worsening pain, a changed stride, or you can't eat and drink, shorten or stop instead of forcing day two.

Pre-trip checklist

0 of 8

Fueling

Start early, stay steady
  • Eat early, before you feel depleted.
  • Start around 30–60 g of carbs per hour; go higher only if you've practiced it.
  • Stick to familiar foods and sports drinks you've tested.
  • Drink to conditions and thirst, with electrolytes as part of a tested plan.
  • Crew carries spare food, socks, blister supplies and warm layers.

Go, carefully

Training done without worsening pain, normal recovery within a day or two, food and fluids sitting well, and access, water, weather and crew confirmed.

Shorten or drop the 48 hr goal

Persistent soreness, heavy legs, trouble fueling, blisters, or uncertain water. A shorter route with reliable exits beats forcing the distance.

Postpone

New injury, illness, pain that changes your stride, or snow, ice, severe weather or closed roads. Get urgent help for confusion, collapse or chest pain.

Trail note: Happy Jack is mostly smooth and fairly flat, but water comes from stock tanks and seasonal drainages that must be treated and may be dry. Check the Arizona Trail Association water report right before you go.

The whole trail, in blocks

About 800 miles, Mexico to Utah, in 9 blocks. Parents drop off and pick up each one.

Winter blocks stay in the low southern desert, and the high country waits for fall and late spring. Aim for 25–30 miles a day, so 100–120 per 4-day block. Mileages are approximate, so get the exact numbers and water sources from FarOut before each trip.

Why this order

Season first
  • The Gila River and Superstition sections are at their best in December and January.
  • The Rincons and Mt. Lemmon climb to 8,000–9,000 ft and hold snow through March, so they wait for April.
  • The North Rim opens May 15, so the Grand Canyon goes last, on Memorial Day.

Book early

Permits & water
  • Grand Canyon backcountry permit for camping below the rim. Apply about 4 months ahead, around January.
  • Saguaro National Park permit if you camp in the Rincons (Manning Camp).
  • Water caches on the Christmas and New Year's blocks. Parents drop gallons at road crossings on the drive in.

Trail food

Light, calorie-dense, tested on long runs first.

Target 4,000–4,500 calories a day at 125+ calories per ounce. That's 4,500 ÷ 125 = 36 oz, or about 2.25 lb of food a day. Pack salty food for the desert, and eat something every hour.

Breakfast

≈ 600–800 cal
  • Instant oatmeal with powdered whole milk and walnuts
  • Pop-Tarts (eat on the move)
  • Instant coffee packets
  • Cold-soaked granola with powdered milk

On the move

≈ 2,000–2,500 cal
  • Tortillas with peanut butter or Nutella
  • Fritos and peanut M&Ms (about 160 and 140 cal/oz)
  • Snickers and ProBars
  • Honey Stinger waffles or gels for climbs
  • Dried mango, trail mix
  • Electrolyte drink mix in every liter

Dinner

≈ 900–1,200 cal
  • Ramen with an olive-oil packet (about 240 cal/oz)
  • Knorr Sides with a tuna or chicken pouch
  • Idahoan mashed potatoes with crushed Fritos
  • Cold-soak couscous with pesto and summer sausage
  • One Peak Refuel per block as a morale meal

Parents at pickup

Real food at the car
  • Burritos, pizza or a sandwich
  • Broth or soup when it's cold
  • Cold soda and chocolate milk
  • The next block's food bags, already packed

Packing rules

Keep it simple
  • One zip bag per day, labeled by day
  • Repackage out of heavy boxes
  • Nothing new on trail; test every food on a long run first
  • A spare 500 cal per day in case a block runs long

The route, 2027 → 2032

Tentative. Starts May 2027 and ends before the 30th birthday on Sep 3, 2032. Trips are grouped by season and region to save PTO and flights. The build runs from climbs to marathons, then 100-milers, then Badwater.

Months are each race's usual window. Check every year's calendar and sign up early for lotteries and sell-outs (Leadville, Cocodona, MdS, Comrades). Badwater needs three finished 100-milers, one within the previous 18 months: Leadville '28, Cocodona '29 and The Border '31 cover that.
